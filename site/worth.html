Are you done being the one they hand it to because you'll say yes? Are you done working this hard for someone who only ever says thank you? Are you done knowing you could earn more somewhere else, and staying anyway? Are you happy?

I'm not going to explain your worth to you. You already know. That's the strange part, and it's why none of the advice lands.

So here are the four questions I actually sat with at 34, in a job I was very good at and being paid almost nothing to be good at. They're the reason I left. They took about ten minutes.

Send me the four questions

I was exactly where you are. That's the only qualification I've got.

For years I made other people's businesses more money than they'd ever made. I got told I was amazing. I got thank yous. I did not get paid like it. And I stayed, because at least they liked me.

Nobody values you into leaving.

I run my own company now. My first $10K month came at 34. I live where I chose to live, doing work that belongs to me. The distance between those two versions of me was never confidence. It was finally putting a number on what I already knew how to do.

Two things you're already thinking.

"I'll just be the same person afterward."
Probably, if it's another course you buy and never open. That's why this is four questions and not a curriculum. You can answer them tonight. Nothing to schedule, nothing to keep up with, no module two.
"That's fine for her. She's different."
I was the person who made the schedule everyone else got credit for. I was the one who said yes to things I didn't want so nobody would be disappointed in me. I am not built differently. I just eventually got tired enough to ask myself something honest.

The four questions I asked myself before I quit.

Not affirmations. Not a vision board. The four I actually sat with, in the order I asked them, that turned a vague bad feeling into something I could act on. Here's the first one, so you know what kind of questions these are.

  • OneWhat am I doing here that nobody else in this building can do? Write the list. Not the job description, the actual list.
  • Two. What staying has already cost me · in the email
  • Three. What I'd have to believe to walk · in the email
  • Four. The one I still can't ask out loud · in the email

One email. No course, no upsell, and you can leave any time.

Some people read the four and move. Most don't, and that isn't a character flaw. Doing it alone is harder than anyone says out loud. When reading stops being enough, I do this with people one on one. It's finite, it has a graduation, and I have no interest in being anyone's permanent crutch.

I wanted to see the world. So I went and built a life that let me.
